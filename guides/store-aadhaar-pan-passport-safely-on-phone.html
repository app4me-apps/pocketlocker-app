Where to safely keep Aadhaar, PAN and passport copies on your phone

Updated 27 September 2026 · Pocket Locker Guides

Quick answer Don't keep ID copies loose in your Gallery, WhatsApp chats or email. Keep them in one app that locks with your fingerprint or Face ID, use a masked Aadhaar when you have to share a copy, and keep a backup you control so a lost phone doesn't mean lost documents.

Aadhaar, PAN, passport, voter ID — we photograph them for every form, KYC and SIM card. Months later those photos are scattered across the Gallery, "sent to self" WhatsApp chats, old emails and Downloads. That's a problem twice over: you can't find them when you need them, and anyone who unlocks your phone can.

Where ID copies usually end up — and the risk

WhereThe risk
Phone GalleryVisible to anyone who picks up your unlocked phone, and often auto-uploaded to cloud photo backup and shown in "memories".
WhatsApp "note to self"Mixed in with thousands of messages; appears in chat backups; one wrong forward shares it.
Email attachmentsIf your email is ever compromised, every ID you ever emailed is in one searchable place.
Downloads folderAny app with file access can read it.

A safer way to keep your ID documents

  1. Keep one copy of each ID in a single locked place — an app that opens only with your fingerprint, Face ID or phone lock.
  2. Delete the scattered copies from your Gallery, WhatsApp and Downloads once they're saved safely.
  3. Use a masked Aadhaar for sharing. UIDAI lets you download a masked Aadhaar that shows only the last 4 digits of your number, from myaadhaar.uidai.gov.in ("Do you want a masked Aadhaar?").
  4. Note the important numbers (passport number, expiry date, PAN) so you can fill forms without opening the photo each time.
  5. Keep a backup you control, so losing or changing your phone doesn't mean losing your IDs.
  6. Track expiry dates — passports and some cards expire, and renewals take weeks.
For official use, government apps like DigiLocker hold verified copies issued directly by the department. A vault app like Pocket Locker is for keeping your own copies organised and private — use both.

Frequently asked questions

Is it safe to keep an Aadhaar photo in my phone Gallery?

It is risky: anyone with your unlocked phone can see it, and Gallery photos are often backed up to the cloud automatically. Keep it in a locked app instead and remove the Gallery copy.

What is a masked Aadhaar?

A version of your Aadhaar that shows only the last 4 digits of the Aadhaar number. You can download it from UIDAI's website (myaadhaar.uidai.gov.in) and share it where the full number is not required.

Is Pocket Locker the same as DigiLocker?

No. DigiLocker holds official, verified copies issued by government departments. Pocket Locker is a private vault for your own copies, numbers, passwords and reminders, organised the way you like. Many people use both.

What happens to my documents if I lose my phone?

With Pocket Locker, if you turned on Google Drive backup, you can restore everything on a new phone. Without a backup, documents stored only on a lost phone cannot be recovered.
